Reporting Dataset and Threat Counts

To support transparency and reproducibility, we explicitly report dataset characteristics and the number of derived threat instances observed during analysis.

Dataset Characterization

For each experimental scenario, we report the total number of identity events, the time span covered, the number of identities observed, and the domains involved (e.g., enterprise IT, cloud, CI/CD, OT). These metrics describe the structural properties of the dataset and are not intended to measure security risk.

Scenario-Driven Evaluation

Scenarios are intentionally constructed to surface specific identity risk patterns (e.g., transitive delegation, orphan non-human identities, delayed blast radius). Reporting scenario-level statistics makes explicit which structural behaviors are being evaluated and avoids reliance on implicit or cherry-picked examples.

Threat Instance Counts

Threat counts are reported at specific points in time to demonstrate the temporal emergence and resolution of identity paths. A threat instance corresponds to a graph pattern that satisfies a predefined structural condition at a given time, such as the existence of a bounded path between an enterprise identity and an OT device.

Importantly, these counts do not represent detection accuracy, incident frequency, or likelihood of compromise. They reflect only the number of graph-derived interpretations valid at the selected time.

Rationale

By reporting counts alongside temporal replay, we enable independent verification that:

Threats are derived from identity paths rather than static assignments

Threats appear and disappear as relationships overlap or expire

Results are deterministic and reproducible under event replay

This approach prioritizes explainability and structural reasoning over predictive performance.
