Research Motivation
Traditional identity and access management tools primarily answer the question:"Who has access right now?" but fail to capture the historical context that matters for security.

Real incidents exploit historical or transient identity paths that may not be visible in current permission snapshots. Attackers leverage expired credentials, revoked trust relationships, and temporary access grants that were never properly decommissioned.

Non-human identities (tokens, service accounts, certificates, OT identities) require different modeling approaches because they operate at different time scales, have different lifecycle patterns, and are often managed outside traditional IAM systems.

This research explores a temporal, graph-based approach to identity analysis where time is treated as a first-class dimension and identity relationships are modeled deterministically.

Research Contribution
This research prototype makes the following contributions:

Temporal Identity Graph model: A graph-based representation of identities and relationships with explicit temporal bounds (valid_from, valid_to) that captures how access evolves over time.
Event-sourced, replayable identity analysis: An append-only event store that enables deterministic reconstruction of identity state at any point in time.
Deterministic risk annotation: Risk annotations that are computed from explicit rules and do not mutate the underlying graph.
Policy evaluation with explicit evidence: An OPA-like policy engine that provides explanations for access decisions with traceable evidence paths.
Focus on explainability over prediction: The system prioritizes understanding why access was granted rather than predicting future behavior.
This research prototype is not production-ready and does not provide defensive guarantees. It is intended for research, education, and analytical experimentation only.
